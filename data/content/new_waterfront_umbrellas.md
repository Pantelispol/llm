## History

The Umbrellas are a large contemporary public sculpture on Thessaloniki’s New Waterfront. Their repeated slender poles and tilted umbrella forms have become a modern visual symbol of the city, complementing the much older White Tower farther along the shore. The work is experienced as part of the promenade rather than inside a separate cultural venue.

## What to see

The sculpture changes with viewpoint, weather, and light. From a distance the umbrellas create a delicate line against the sea and sky; close up, overlapping poles produce dense geometric patterns. Reflections and silhouettes are especially noticeable around changing daylight. The open setting also gives wide views across the Thermaic Gulf and back toward the waterfront skyline.

## Practical tips

The site is fully outdoors, exposed to sun, wind, rain, and heat reflected from the promenade. Cyclists, runners, and pedestrians share the surrounding waterfront, so stopping for photographs should not block the path. The broad route is relatively flat, although surface conditions and temporary barriers can change. The sculpture combines easily with the White Tower, the nearby museums, or a longer walk through the Nea Paralia parks. After dark, personal safety and lighting should be assessed like any open public waterfront.
