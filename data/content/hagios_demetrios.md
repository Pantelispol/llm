## History

Dedicated to the patron saint of Thessaloniki, the basilica of Hagios Demetrios is a premier pilgrimage site and part of the UNESCO-listed Paleochristian and Byzantine monuments of Thessaloniki. Originally founded in the early fifth century (412–413 CE) by the prefect Leontios, the church was reconstructed as a grand five-aisled basilica in the seventh century (629–634 CE) following a devastating fire. It is renowned for its rare surviving pre-iconoclastic mosaics dating from the fifth to seventh centuries, depicting Saint Demetrius with church patrons and children. Converted to a mosque in 1493, the basilica suffered catastrophic damage in the Great Fire of 1917 before being meticulously restored.

## What to see

The broad basilica interior is organized by rows of columns and opens toward richly decorated sacred spaces. Surviving mosaics, carved architectural elements, and the scale of the nave convey the church’s long religious importance. Below the church, the crypt preserves structures associated with Roman baths and the traditions surrounding Saint Demetrius. Together, church and crypt show how worship developed around an older urban setting.

## Practical tips

As an active place of worship, modest dress and quiet conduct are expected, especially during services. Ceremonies can limit interior access without notice. The church sits uphill from the waterfront within the central street grid. Visitors with mobility needs should confirm accessible entrances and crypt access.
