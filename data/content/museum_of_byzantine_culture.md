## History

The Museum of Byzantine Culture explores the Byzantine and post-Byzantine worlds through objects from Thessaloniki and the surrounding region. Its galleries place religious art alongside evidence of urban life, burial, craft, trade, and the changing communities of a major eastern Mediterranean city. The museum complements Thessaloniki’s surviving churches and monuments by presenting movable objects in historical context.

## What to see

The collections include mosaics, wall paintings, sculpture, icons, manuscripts, coins, ceramics, and objects used in daily life. Displays explain both artistic techniques and the social settings in which objects were made and used. Reconstructed architectural contexts help visitors connect fragments in the museum with churches and buildings encountered elsewhere in the city.

## Practical tips

The galleries are indoors and reward a focused visit built around a few themes, especially if several Byzantine churches are also on the itinerary. Travelers with mobility needs should verify the current accessible route and available seating. The Archaeological Museum is nearby, while the White Tower and waterfront are also within a compact area. Visiting the museum before the churches can provide useful vocabulary for recognizing mosaics, icons, marble carving, and changes in Byzantine visual culture.
