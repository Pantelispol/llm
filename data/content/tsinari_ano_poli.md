## History

Tsinari is a small neighborhood area within Ano Poli, Thessaloniki’s Upper Town. The district preserves a street scale and building pattern distinct from the denser modern center below. Its name is associated with the area’s Ottoman past, while the surrounding lanes reflect the long continuity and gradual change of residential life on the hillside.

## What to see

The appeal is the neighborhood itself: narrow streets, small houses, tiled roofs, traditional façades, corners opening toward city views, and an intimate public space around the local square. Architectural details are mixed rather than uniform, and everyday residential use is part of what visitors encounter. Nearby slopes connect Tsinari visually and physically with the walls and other parts of Ano Poli.

## Practical tips

Tsinari is an area, not a single managed attraction. Residents’ privacy should be respected, and individual businesses have their own conditions. Routes from the center are uphill and may include steep grades, steps, uneven paving, and limited shade. The area can serve as a rest point when a route already passes through Ano Poli, but sending visitors uphill only for a meal adds avoidable travel. The walls, Vlatadon Monastery, and Hagios Demetrios can be combined selectively, with enough time allowed for slopes.
