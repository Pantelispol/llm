## History

The Archaeological Museum of Thessaloniki presents material from ancient Macedonia and the wider region. Its collections trace communities from prehistory through the Roman era, placing the city within the political, artistic, and commercial networks of northern Greece. The museum building and displays are separate from the archaeological sites where many objects were found.

## What to see

Visitors encounter sculpture, metalwork, pottery, funerary objects, architectural fragments, and finds connected with public and private life. Gold objects and richly worked grave goods demonstrate the skill and resources of ancient Macedonian society, while inscriptions and everyday artifacts give a broader view beyond rulers and warfare. Thematic displays help connect individual objects with burial customs, cities, trade, and belief.

## Practical tips

This is a substantial indoor collection, so choosing a few themes is often more rewarding than trying to inspect every case. Seating and the exact step-free route should be confirmed for individual needs. The museum lies near the Museum of Byzantine Culture, the White Tower, and the waterfront, making those places easy to combine without crossing the whole city.
