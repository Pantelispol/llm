## History

Dedicated to the Holy Wisdom of God, Hagia Sophia is one of the most significant Byzantine churches in central Thessaloniki and a UNESCO World Heritage site. Constructed in the late eighth century (c. 780–797 CE) over the remains of a massive fifth-century Roman and early Christian basilica, it stands as an outstanding transitional example of a domed cross-in-square church. The monument is internationally renowned for its exceptional Byzantine mosaics, including the monumental late ninth-century (c. 885 CE) Ascension of Christ decorating the main dome and the iconic Virgin and Child in the sanctuary apse. Converted into a mosque in 1593 under Ottoman rule, the church was restored to Christian worship in 1912.

## What to see

The building combines a compact central plan with a prominent dome. Inside, visitors can observe Byzantine mosaics, painted decoration, marble elements, and the way light moves across the elevated surfaces. The layered fabric reflects changes in worship and conservation over many centuries. Outside, the church sits within a leafy urban square that gives space to view its brickwork and overall form despite the dense surrounding streets.

## Practical tips

Hagia Sophia is an active church. The square offers shade and a useful pause during central walking routes. Acheiropoietos, the Arch of Galerius, Aristotelous Square, and the waterfront are easily reachable on foot. Visitors requiring step-free access should check entrance points in advance.
