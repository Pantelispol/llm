## History

Bey Hamam is an Ottoman bath complex in the center of Thessaloniki. Built during the early Ottoman period, it represents the hammam as both an urban service and a social institution. The building was designed with separate bathing sequences and rooms that moved users through changing temperatures, supported by water and heating systems hidden within the structure.

## What to see

From outside, the clustered domes and masonry volumes reveal the organization of the bath. Small openings in the domes once brought patterned light into the interior spaces. Where interior access is available, visitors can read the progression from reception and changing areas toward warmer bathing rooms, along with surviving plaster, decorative detail, and the engineering required to heat the building.

## Practical tips

Interior access can depend on conservation work or cultural use, so the exterior should be treated as the dependable descriptive experience unless current official information confirms more. The monument stands next to a busy central plaza and can be viewed as part of a short route linking the Roman Forum, Kapani Market, and Aristotelous Square. Traffic, crowds, and urban paving may affect the approach. Visitors should not infer accessibility from the surrounding pedestrian space because historic thresholds and interior level changes may differ.
