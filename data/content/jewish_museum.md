## History

The Jewish Museum of Thessaloniki documents a community that shaped the city’s commercial, social, and cultural life for centuries. It gives particular attention to the Sephardic population, the destruction caused by the Holocaust, and the survival of memory after most of Thessaloniki’s Jewish residents were deported and murdered during the Second World War.

## What to see

The displays bring together photographs, documents, religious objects, family material, and architectural fragments. They describe synagogues, schools, workplaces, neighborhoods, language, and communal institutions, making the prewar city visible through individual lives as well as statistics. Material concerning persecution and deportation connects local places with the wider history of the Holocaust. The museum also explains how records and objects were preserved by later generations.

## Practical tips

The subject matter is emotionally demanding and benefits from unhurried attention. Families may wish to prepare younger visitors for testimony and images related to persecution and mass murder. Security procedures and accessibility arrangements can change, so visitors with specific needs should consult the current official source. The museum is in the historic commercial center, close to Modiano Market, Kapani Market, Ladadika, and Aristotelous Square. Its content adds essential context to routes that might otherwise focus only on Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman heritage.
