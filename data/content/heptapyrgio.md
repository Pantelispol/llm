## History

Dominating the highest point of the upper city (Ano Poli), Heptapyrgio (historically known as Yenti Koule) is the fortified citadel and acropolis of Thessaloniki. The complex preserves a clear multi-period sequence: its northern perimeter incorporates late Byzantine wall towers dating from the twelfth to fourteenth centuries (with documented fourteenth-century Palaiologan repairs), while the southern enclosure and seven-tower fortification were constructed in 1431 CE by the Ottoman governor Sungur Chaush Bey shortly after the 1430 conquest. In the late nineteenth century (c. 1890s), the military compound was converted into an infamous prison that remained operational until 1989, adding a poignant modern chapter to its medieval and Ottoman defensive history.

## What to see

The enclosure, gates, towers, and heavy masonry show how the citadel controlled the upper defenses of the city. Different building techniques are visible across the fortifications. From the surrounding high ground, visitors can relate Heptapyrgio to the Ano Poli walls, the lower city, and the Thermaic Gulf. Interior spaces associated with the former prison give the site a more somber character than a conventional panoramic viewpoint.

## Practical tips

Heptapyrgio stands on steep terrain, and approaches from the center involve a sustained uphill journey. Uneven ground, stairs, and exposed areas can be difficult in heat or poor weather. Comfortable footwear and water are sensible, while travelers with children or reduced mobility should assess the route conservatively. The nearby walls and Ano Poli lanes can be combined with the visit, but adding them increases both climbing and outdoor exposure.
