## History

The Roman Forum preserves part of the civic center of ancient Thessaloniki. Public administration, commerce, performance, and social life were concentrated around this multi-level complex during the Roman period. The remains sit below the level of the modern city, making the archaeological site a visible reminder that later Thessaloniki was built directly over earlier urban layers.

## What to see

Visitors can examine the outlines of public buildings, paved areas, columns, and the small theater-like odeon. Subterranean passages and displayed finds help explain how the complex was organized across changing ground levels. Views from the surrounding streets are useful for understanding the forum’s overall footprint before descending into the site. The nearby modern blocks emphasize how centrally the ancient civic space was positioned.

## Practical tips

Much of the visit is outdoors, with limited protection from strong sun or rain. Archaeological surfaces may include steps, slopes, and uneven paving, so the current accessible route should be confirmed when step-free access matters. Allow time to look down on the ruins as well as walking among them. Hagios Demetrios, the central markets, and Aristotelous Square are natural nearby continuations for a history-focused route.
