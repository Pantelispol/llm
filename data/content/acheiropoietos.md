## History

The Church of the Acheiropoietos is an early Christian basilica and one of the oldest surviving churches in Thessaloniki. Its long history includes Byzantine worship and later use as a mosque during the Ottoman period. The building is part of the city’s UNESCO-listed collection of Paleochristian and Byzantine monuments and remains an active Orthodox church.

## What to see

The interior preserves the clear proportions of a timber-roofed basilica, with columns dividing the central nave from side aisles. Marble architectural elements, capitals, floor remains, and fragments of mosaic decoration reveal the craftsmanship of its early phases. The relatively restrained space makes the ancient structure easy to read, while later religious furnishings show that it continues to function as a living church.

## Practical tips

Visitors should wear modest clothing, keep voices low, and pause rather than moving through areas being used for worship. Services may change how freely the interior can be explored. The church lies in the dense center between the Roman Forum, Hagia Sophia, and the Galerian monuments, so it fits naturally into a walking route. Pavement and thresholds may matter for wheelchairs or strollers; travelers needing step-free access should confirm the most suitable entrance in advance.
