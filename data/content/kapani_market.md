## History

Kapani is one of Thessaloniki’s traditional central markets. Its lanes have long connected everyday food commerce with the surrounding commercial district. Rather than functioning as a single enclosed hall, the market spreads through a network of narrow passages and shopfronts, retaining a direct relationship with the streets around it.

## What to see

The market’s character comes from closely packed displays, signs, awnings, delivery activity, and the movement between food stalls and neighboring shops. Produce, spices, preserved goods, meat, fish, and household items contribute different colors, textures, and smells. The compact layout gives a strong impression of daily urban commerce and contrasts with the more formal spaces of nearby Aristotelous Square and the covered Modiano Market.

## Practical tips

Kapani is a working market, so visitors should keep passageways clear, ask before photographing people at close range, and expect wet or uneven surfaces in some sections. Crowds and deliveries can make narrow lanes difficult for wheelchairs or strollers. Conditions vary between individual stalls and should be checked independently when a specific stop matters. The market works best as part of a central walk that also includes the Roman Forum, Bey Hamam, Modiano Market, or the Jewish Museum.
