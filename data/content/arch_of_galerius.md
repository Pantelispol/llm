## History

The Arch of Galerius is a surviving part of a Roman triumphal monument constructed between 298 and 305 CE (early fourth century) during the Roman Tetrarchy to commemorate the campaign against the Persians. It formed an integral section of the imperial ceremonial route connecting the palace complex with the Rotunda. Thessalonians commonly call it Kamara, and the monument remains both an archaeological landmark and an everyday meeting point in the center of the city.

## What to see

Carved relief panels cover the surviving piers. They present imperial ceremonies, soldiers, captives, and episodes connected with Galerius’s campaigns against Persia. The arch is fragmentary, so looking across the modern street helps reconstruct the scale of the original multi-arched structure and its place on the Roman processional axis. The contrast between detailed Roman stonework, apartment buildings, and constant pedestrian movement is part of the experience.

## Practical tips

This is an outdoor monument in an active public space rather than a self-contained interior visit. Shade is limited immediately around the arch, and traffic, bicycles, and busy crossings require attention. The site is a short walk from the Rotunda and works well as a concise stop between the upper center and the waterfront. The reliefs are easiest to appreciate by circling the surviving piers without touching the stone.
