## History

Nea Paralia is Thessaloniki’s renewed waterfront landscape southeast of the White Tower. A sequence of themed gardens and public spaces reshaped the shoreline into a long recreational corridor. The project connects everyday exercise, play, planting, and views of the Thermaic Gulf rather than presenting one enclosed park or historic monument.

## What to see

Walking along the promenade reveals changing garden rooms, lawns, planted areas, seating, play spaces, and open decks beside the sea. The sequence is more important than any single feature: dense vegetation alternates with exposed waterfront, while public art and city views provide landmarks. The Umbrellas sculpture forms one of the best-known focal points along the route.

## Practical tips

The waterfront is generally flat and suitable for a flexible walk, but it covers a long distance and facilities vary between sections. Choose a clear meeting point or garden rather than treating the entire park system as one compact stop. Exposed portions offer little shade and can be uncomfortable during strong heat, wind, or storms. Cyclists and faster pedestrians share parts of the route. Families may value the open spaces, but supervision remains important near bicycle movement and the water’s edge. The White Tower and museum district provide natural northern endpoints.
