## History

The walls of Thessaloniki protected the city through late Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman periods. Large stretches survive around Ano Poli, where the fortifications follow the high ground above the modern center. Towers, gates, and repaired sections record centuries of adaptation rather than a single construction campaign.

## What to see

The upper walls offer broad views across tiled roofs, the lower city, and the Thermaic Gulf. Close inspection reveals changes in masonry, reused stone, brick bands, towers, and the relationship between defensive structures and the neighborhood streets. The walls are best understood as a long urban landscape: some sections are managed monuments, while others are exterior remains seen from public paths and viewpoints.

## Practical tips

Routes around the walls are hilly and can include steep lanes, steps, uneven paving, and stretches with little shade. Public viewpoints do not guarantee lighting or staffed access, so daylight is the safer setting for an unaccompanied walk. Heat, storms, and slippery surfaces materially change the difficulty. Choose a specific viewpoint rather than treating the entire wall circuit as one stop. Heptapyrgio, Tsinari, and Vlatadon Monastery are nearby, but combining them requires additional climbing.
